# Kick-off results (offline arm, LoCoMo, 18 Oct 2026)

Status: **pipeline runs end to end; numbers below are the first offline pass, not paper numbers.**
No model is called at the gate: extraction uses LoCoMo's released observations (gpt-3.5-turbo, produced with the whole session visible) through a
deterministic sentence-to-triple rule, retrieval is BM25 + 1-hop, and "value" is evidence recall
(does the retrieved set contain a fact sourced from a gold evidence turn), not answer F1.

## Setup

| item | value |
|---|---|
| data | LoCoMo `locomo10.json`, 10 conversations, 1,986 questions |
| split | fit: conv-26, conv-30, conv-41, conv-42, conv-43 (1,129 candidate turns) · test: conv-44, conv-47, conv-48, conv-49, conv-50 (987 questions) |
| candidates | one candidate set per turn from the oracle observations; 1 to 5 triples |
| memory | `paper2.graph.MemoryGraph` on `cgraph.ContextGraph`; RapidFuzz ER at 90; consolidation per session |
| gate | online quantile with budget q; tie rule, proportional control and a ±0.05 budget band (`paper2.policies.OnlineQuantile`) |
| retrieval | BM25 top-20 + 1-hop expansion, cap 40 |
| scoring | evidence-hit (any gold evidence turn's fact retrieved), frac (share of gold turns covered), per category; 95% bootstrap CI over questions |

Everything below is regenerated by `sh paper2/sweep.sh` (about four minutes, no model calls); the
individual commands are

```
.venv/bin/python -m paper2.run run --dataset locomo --policy admit_all --offline --conv conv-26 conv-30 conv-41 conv-42 conv-43 --name fit_train_admit_all
.venv/bin/python -m paper2.run fit --features paper2/out/fit_train_admit_all/turns.jsonl --out paper2/out/gate_weights.json
.venv/bin/python -m paper2.run run --dataset locomo --policy graph_gate --weights paper2/out/gate_weights.json --offline --budget 0.5 --conv conv-44 conv-47 conv-48 conv-49 conv-50
.venv/bin/python -m paper2.analysis table "paper2/out/test_*/summary.json"
.venv/bin/python -m paper2.analysis features paper2/out/fit_train_admit_all/turns.jsonl
```

## 1. Degeneracy of the 2010 measures (confirmed; mechanism partly extraction-made, see critique B(iv))

On the fit split, `T_edge = |Et ∩ Ec| / |Et|` is exactly 0 on 89.3% of candidate turns and 1 on
0.5%; it takes 6 distinct values. `SIVp(2010) = T_edge × I` is therefore 0 on 89.3% of turns. As a
ranking signal it is below random (table 3): it can only prefer candidates that *repeat* memory.
This is the reviewer objection from the proposal round, now measured rather than argued.

Reading: being *about already-known things* (anchoring, soft agreement, 2010 Trust) is **negatively**
associated with value on LoCoMo. The pre-registered prior weights had the sign right for `t_soft`
and `t_edge` and wrong for `anchoring`. A logistic gate over all twelve features reaches grouped
5-fold AUC 0.673 (folds 0.63 to 0.73); its largest weights are text_len (+0.36), n_edges (−0.34),
t_soft (−0.31), recency (+0.30).

Caveat that must go in the paper: the label is a proxy. "Evidence turn for some question" is what
LoCoMo annotators chose to ask about, which correlates with turn length and specificity. The real
test is retrieval/QA under a memory budget (table 3), where the label is never used by any policy.

What this says, and no more:
- **Every structural gate beats random and the 2010 product** in both directions, by 6 to 12 points
  at half the memory, with Holm-corrected p < 0.001 and 4 or 5 of 5 conversations positive.
- **No structural gate reliably beats the surface heuristics** (heat, A-MAC-lite) on five test
  conversations: the point estimates are positive in 7 of 8 comparisons but the cluster intervals
  include zero in all but one. Impact alone is beaten in one direction. The honest statement is
  "matches or slightly exceeds"; the 10-conversation study cannot settle it, LongMemEval and the
  online arm must.
- **The unfitted prior gate is the best structural policy in one direction and the fitted
  structural + surface gate in the other**; the fitted structural-only gate is never best. The
  logistic fit to the "evidence turn" label is not optimising the retrieval target, and the label
  is a proxy. Fitting directly to retrieval value (or treating the prior gate as the structural
  policy and the fit as a check) is the better design.
- Admit rates are now within ±0.05 of the budget for every policy, but `live` still differs by up to
  15% because turns carry 1 to 5 triples; the budget curve, not a single q, is the fair comparison.

## 4. Poisoning arm (H4): structure is blind to truth, provenance is not

10% of candidate turns receive one fabricated functional fact about a speaker (random object from
the corpus vocabulary), carried at tier `llm-summary` (confidence 0.6) versus 0.9 for oracle
candidates. The policy never sees `truth`. Evaluated on B with gates fitted on A, q = 0.5, 303
poisoned candidates.

| policy | tier multiplier | poison admitted | evidence-hit |
|---|---|---|---|
| random | no | 146 / 303 (48%) | 0.405 |
| random | yes | 107 / 303 (35%) | 0.427 |
| graph_gate structural-only | no | **169 / 303 (56%)** | 0.423 |
| graph_gate structural-only | yes | **43 / 303 (14%)** | 0.444 |
| graph_gate prior | no | 177 / 303 (58%) | 0.468 |
| graph_gate prior | yes | 43 / 303 (14%) | 0.509 |
| graph_gate structural + surface | no | 110 / 303 (36%) | 0.477 |
| graph_gate structural + surface | yes | 61 / 303 (20%) | 0.510 |

A fabricated fact is, structurally, a novel fact about a known person with a rare predicate: exactly
what a structural gate prefers, so it admits *more* poison than random (56 to 58% vs 48%). The
pre-registered tier multiplier (score × mean tier confidence) cuts poison admission four-fold at
the same admit rate and raises evidence-hit by 2 to 4 points, because poison stops displacing real
facts. For the paper: structural value and provenance trust are orthogonal signals; their product
is the admission score, which is the 2010 paper's `t × deg(v)` weighting read as a policy. The
caveat is that the tier here is a label the pipeline assigns by extraction route; an attacker who
can forge the route defeats it.

## 5. Consolidation almost never fires with this extractor (matters for H2 and H3)

After removing the copula `is` from the functional predicate set (it produced 22 to 52 false
"contradictions" per conversation such as "is managing kids" vs "is going swimming"), the
functional rule invalidated **1 fact in 5 conversations**. Consequently SIVa with consolidation and
the plain new-triple ratio are nearly the same quantity here (AUC 0.568 vs 0.562), and the section-11
consolidation claim (consolidation-aware SIVa differs from the plain ratio) cannot be tested on this arm. The deterministic
rule yields verb lemmas ("value", "attend", "went"), not normalised profile predicates
(`lives_in`, `works_at`), so functional conflicts are invisible to it. The LLM extraction arm with
a fixed predicate vocabulary is where H3 must be tested; LongMemEval's knowledge-update questions
are the natural stress test.

## 6. Other observations

- Oracle candidates cover 76% of gold evidence turns (admit-all recall 0.76); 24% of the
  evidence is unreachable by any admission policy in this arm. Admit-all evidence-hit (0.652) is the
  ceiling for all policies.
- LongMemEval path runs (`--dataset longmemeval --extractor heuristic`, 20 questions: evidence-hit
  0.70 under admit-all); the heuristic extractor's admission precision is 0.11 to 0.20, so the LLM
  extraction arm is needed before LongMemEval numbers mean anything.
- Entity typing from the rule is coarse: 173 `Thing`, 12 `NamedEntity`, 2 `Person` in conv-26.
  Pattern novelty therefore reduces to predicate novelty. An NER pass (spaCy) would fix this
  cheaply and is LLM-free.
- Runtime: about 0.3 s per conversation per policy; the whole sweep in this file took under two
  minutes on one CPU. The write path made 0 model calls.

## 7. What changes in the plan because of this

1. Headline policy = **structural gate × tier confidence**; report the structural-only fit, the prior
   gate and the full (structural + surface) gate separately so the claim stays about structure.
2. Claim to defend: "LLM-free structural admission beats random and the 2010 product by a wide
   margin, matches surface heuristics, and with provenance weighting is far more poison-resistant
   than either." Do not claim superiority over heat / A-MAC-lite until the 10-conversation
   cross-fitted run (fit on 5, test on 5, both directions) and LongMemEval agree.
3. Report anchoring and soft agreement as **novelty-type** signals (negative sign) and say so in the
   measures section: on conversational memory, redundancy with memory predicts low value.
4. Treat the 2010 product as a negative result with a mechanism (89% zeros), not as a baseline.
5. Move H3 to the LLM-extraction arm with a closed predicate vocabulary; add LongMemEval
   knowledge-update questions as the primary H3 test bed.
6. Before any claim: the online arm (fixed answerer, F1/EM) on the same memories, so that evidence
   recall is validated against answer quality at least once.
